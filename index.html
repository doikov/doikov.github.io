Welcome! I work on optimization for machine learning and AI.

I am an assistant professor in the School of Operations Research and Information Engineering (ORIE) at Cornell University.

My research combines algorithm design with convergence and complexity analysis. I am interested in how problem structure, curvature, and randomness can make training large AI models more efficient.

I received my PhD in 2021 at UCLouvain, Belgium, supervised by Yurii Nesterov. My thesis is New second-order and tensor methods in Convex Optimization. After that, I was a postdoctoral researcher at EPFL, Switzerland, working in the Machine Learning and Optimization Laboratory with Martin Jaggi.

Recent Research Directions

All papers ↓

Accelerated and adaptive Newton methods

Fast Newton methods with global guarantees and simple updates. This includes primal acceleration with one linear solve per iteration, global linear convergence for quasi-self-concordant objectives, and adaptive super-universal methods.

Primal acceleration · 2026 Quasi-self-concordance · Math. Prog. 2025 Super-universal Newton · SIOPT 2024

Curvature, non-convexity, and geometry of training

How problem geometry affects optimization, and how to exploit it through global curvature bounds, affine-invariant methods, and inexpensive Hessian information.

Global curvature bounds · 2026 Lazy Hessians · ICML 2023 Graded non-convexity · ICML 2024 Contracting Newton · NeurIPS 2020

Shuffling, stochasticity, and data unlearning

Convergence and complexity guarantees for shuffled and stochastic optimization, and for removing the influence of training data through certified unlearning.

Certified unlearning · ICML Workshop 2026 Momentum · AISTATS 2025 Variance reduction · TMLR 2024 Shuffling & data ordering · ICML 2024

Collaborative and causal learning

Collaborative work on personalized on-device large language models (LLMs) with mixtures of experts (CoMiGS), and causal discovery from interventional data (PACER).

CoMiGS · ICML 2025 PACER · ICML 2026
